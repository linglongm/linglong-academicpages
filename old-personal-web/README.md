## Past Projects

- Compiling Structured Operational Semantics to Executable OCaml Code - I added a module to the Sterling compiler developed by Dr. Dawn Michaelson. The extension translates structured operational semantics into OCaml, supporting both small-step and big-step semantics and both dynamic and static rules such as type checking. It is part of the [Modular Metatheory for Extensible Languages](https://mmel.cs.umn.edu/) project.
- I worked on a Rust-like ownership language compiler built on top of CompCertO, which extends CompCert, a formally verified C compiler written in Rocq. I primarily  verified a pass from RustIR to Clight, a small-step intermediate language subset of C, using backward simulation. I proved theorems such as one that translates any RustIR expression into a Clight expression while maintaining an injection relation between the memory blocks of the two program states. [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10015)

<summary>Abstract</summary>

We have been examining the historiography of the Anpo uprising—specifically, the fundamental approaches scholars have taken in writing about it. We found two books on this topic: <em>Protest in Tokyo: The Security Treaty Crisis of 1960</em> (1966) by George R. Packard III, an assistant to Reischauer (one of the founders of Japan's modernization theory), and <em>Japan at the Crossroads</em> (2018) by Nick Kapur, a professor in the History Department at Rutgers University. We are analyzing these works through a theoretical lens. The narrative differences largely reflect distinct conceptions of the modern nation. <em>Protest in Tokyo</em> is written primarily in what Homi Bhabha calls "pedagogical time"—stable, sequential, and grand in scope. As Bhabha argues, "The people are not simply historical events or parts of a patriotic body politic. They are also a complex signification of cultural difference, constructed through the pedagogical, discursive, and disciplinary processes of the nation." We will demonstrate how <em>Protest in Tokyo</em> is more didactic, attempting to construct a narrative of the new Japanese nation for both American and Japanese audiences, as Packard was deeply influenced by Reischauer and the McCarthyism that contributed to the death of modernization theory's opponent, Herbert Norman.
